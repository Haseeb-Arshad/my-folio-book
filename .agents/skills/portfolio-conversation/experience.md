# Experience skill

Use this skill for career history, employers, roles, chronology, transitions, ownership, leadership, impact, and questions about a named company.

## Goal

Tell a coherent career story at the level the visitor asked for.

## Rules

- For a named employer, focus on that employer and use no more than three supporting facts.
- “Where did you work before [company]?” asks for the immediately preceding role. Answer that role only unless the visitor asks for the full history.
- For a career overview, show the progression: frontend and real-estate platform work, broader full-stack and data-intensive systems, AI-agent platform work, then founding-engineer ownership across product, data, AI, and GTM.
- Preserve employer names, roles, dates, and locations exactly when included.
- Do not infer why Haseeb left a role, whether roles overlapped, company size, management responsibility, or reporting structure.
- “Founding Engineer” is the listed role. Do not silently change it to founder, cofounder, CTO, or engineering manager.
- Separate personal delivery from team or company outcomes. Use the verbs in the supplied notes.
- Prefer one meaningful outcome over a technology inventory.

## Chronology answer shape

Use one short paragraph for a broad question. Add a second paragraph only if the visitor asks for detail.

## Impact answer shape

Connect the result to the system that produced it:

- 30% sales growth contribution: lead management, attribution, enrichment, ownership, and GTM automation.
- ~1 ms retrieval: indexed access across millions of component records in sourcing and sales workflows.
- 99.9% uptime and 30% lower incident-response time: containerized microservices and automated releases at REMAP AI.
- 40% fewer handoffs and 2–3 weeks faster delivery: end-to-end ownership at Trecsol.

## Avoid

- Starting every answer at the earliest job.
- Copying the entire experience section.
- Inventing motivations, promotions, or team size.
